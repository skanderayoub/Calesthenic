## What your numbers actually say

This is the important part, so read it before the tables.

| Movement | Your working sets | Estimated true max |
|---|---|---|
| Pull-ups (strict, pronated) | — | **4** |
| Dips | 5 × 8 = 40 reps | **~12–15** |
| Push-ups | 4 × 10 = 40 reps | **~18–22** |

**Your push is roughly three times ahead of your pull.** That's the headline. Someone who can grind 40 quality dips should be doing 8–10 strict pull-ups, not 4.

This matters for all three of your goals:

- **Shoulder health.** Push-dominant training pulls the shoulders forward and internally rotates them. Left alone for another six months, this is how people end up with impingement — and dips are the exercise that punishes it hardest.
- **Physique.** Your back and biceps are the lagging half. Back width is most of what makes a physique read as "trained" from the front, and it's exactly what a pull deficit starves.
- **Skills.** Every skill you want is pull-gated. Muscle-up, front lever, even the pulling strength behind a clean handstand press. Your dips are already muscle-up ready; your pull-ups are the thing holding the door shut.

**So this version rebalances.** Push work stays where it is — it's clearly recoverable, you're already doing it, and you want the size. But nearly all *new* volume goes to pull. You're not cutting anything. You're catching one half up to the other.

### Also: your first version's loading was too heavy

I had you at 5×4 pull-ups. With a genuine 4-rep max, that's five sets to failure — which is how you'd build elbow tendinopathy in about three weeks. Everything below is recalculated off a 4RM and sits deliberately at 50–75% of it.

## The right/left asymmetry

First, calibrate the worry: **a visible difference between dominant and non-dominant side is close to universal.** Tennis players are the extreme case, but nearly everyone's dominant arm is measurably bigger. Seeing it in the mirror is not evidence of a problem.

What *is* worth checking is whether it's a **strength** difference, because that one compounds. On bilateral exercises the stronger side quietly takes more of the load, so the gap widens the harder you train. This is also why sloppy near-failure reps are risky for you specifically — your last two ugly pull-up reps are almost certainly your right side taking over.

**Test it once, this week:**

| Test | Left | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Single-arm dead hang (seconds) | ___ | ___ |
| Single-arm band row (reps, same band) | ___ | ___ |
| Single-arm Australian row (reps) | ___ | ___ |

**Reading the result:**
- Under 10% difference → normal, ignore it
- 10–20% → the protocol below fixes it over a few months
- Over 20%, or any pain → worth a physio's assessment before you load it heavily

**The protocol, built into every session below:**
1. Every unilateral set **starts with the weaker side**
2. The stronger side **matches the weaker side's reps** — never more, even when it could
3. One extra set for the weaker side only, on row variations
4. Film yourself from the front on pull-ups once a month — check if one shoulder rises first

Do not try to fix this with extra curls on one arm. It's driven by how you distribute load on the big movements.

### Optional accelerator: greasing the groove

If — and only if — you have a bar at home: do **2 pull-ups** (half your max, never more) several times a day, on non-training days too. Never hard, never to failure, just walking past the bar and doing two. This is the single fastest method for adding reps at your level, and at 2 reps it adds almost no recovery cost. Skip it entirely if the only bar you have is at a park.

# BLOCK 2 — Weeks 5–8: Build volume

**Expect at week 5:** 6–7 strict pull-ups.

**PULL A:** Pull-ups 5×3 → 5×4 across the block. Band assistance gets lighter or drops. Rows go weighted (backpack) or to archer variation.

**PULL B — EMOM density.** At the top of each minute, do the reps, rest the remainder.

| Week | EMOM |
|---|---|
| 5 | 10 min × 2 reps (20 total) |
| 6 | 12 min × 2 reps (24) |
| 7 | 10 min × 3 reps (30) |
| 8 | 12 min × 3 reps (36) |

If you can't finish the final minute at the prescribed reps, you started too high — drop one and rebuild.

**PUSH — this is where you add load.** Your dips are ready for it even though your pull-ups aren't. Weighted dips: 4×6 at +2.5 kg, adding 2.5 kg every two weeks. Push-ups progress to decline or pseudo-planche. Handstand moves to freestanding attempts (bail *sideways* — turn and step out, never fold backwards).

**Front lever:** tuck → advanced tuck, 4 × 8–12s.

**Week 8 = consolidation + retest.**

# BLOCK 3 — Weeks 9–12: Load the pull

**Expect at week 9:** 9–10 strict pull-ups. Now the pull side is finally ready for weight.

**The weighted pull-up gate:** 5 × 5 strict bodyweight with two reps still in reserve on the last set. Not before. When you clear it, start at +2.5 kg and add 2.5 kg at a time — at 67 kg that's a ~4% jump, which is plenty.

| 5 | Single-arm row | 3 × 10 each + 1 extra weaker |
| 6 | Face pulls + curls | 3 × 15 / 3 × 12 |

**PULL B:** EMOM 12 × 3–4 · explosive chest-to-bar pull-ups 5 × 3 (power work, fully rested) · rows.

**PUSH:** weighted dips 5 × 5 · pseudo-planche push-ups · freestanding handstand · full L-sit.

**Week 12: full retest**, every number side by side with Day 0.

## Nutrition — brief, because it's simple

At 67 kg, roughly **110–145 g of protein a day**. Skills and size both want a modest calorie surplus — you're light, and gaining a couple of kilos of muscle will help your physique far more than it hurts your pull-ups. Sleep seven to nine hours; it does more for your numbers than any extra set.

---

## Stop and back off if

- Ache on the **inside** of the elbow — the classic overuse injury at your stage, and the one your dip volume plus new pull volume most invites
- Sharp or pinching shoulder pain on dips or pull-ups
- Painful clicking in the shoulder
- The left/right gap visibly *widening* rather than closing
- Rising resting heart rate, worsening sleep, dropping motivation — that's accumulated fatigue; take a light week

Pain that's sharp, localised, or lasts beyond a few days deserves a physio, not a forum.

---

## Three rules that decide whether this works

1. **Stay short of failure.** Almost every set here sits at 50–75% of your max. That's not undertraining — it's the mechanism. Test true maxes every 4 weeks, never weekly.
2. **Log everything.** At your stage progress is 1–2 reps a month. Invisible without a written record, and it *feels* like standing still. It isn't.
3. **Weaker side always goes first, stronger side always matches.** The whole asymmetry fix is that one habit, applied for months.
